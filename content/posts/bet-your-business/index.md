Last Boxing Day (the day after Christmas), I went into the mall and bought a 3D printer on a whim. Some friends in Melbourne had been discussing them for a couple of months, and I couldn’t see the point of owning one, but one friend sent me pictures of things he had made around the house that were actually useful, e.g. some custom curtain rods to fit under his cornice. My wife wanted a $500 giant nutcracker from Costco, and agreed to the purchase if I printed her one with the printer instead.

Fast forward to today, and as with my entire school and uni life, I left this to the last minute. My printer has been printing non-stop for almost the last 3 weeks, as I try to get this nutcracker done in time for Christmas. I thought (naively) that I could just scale up an existing model 300% (as big as I could get on my printer), and the little 40cm model would work. I’m not sure what I was thinking, because clearly 40x3 is nowhere near the 1.8m I was told to get to.

As I am wont to do, I applied AI to the problem, and Claude and ChatGPT redesigned the 3d model, split parts across multiple plates, added dovetail joints for strength, and threaded supports for metal rods to keep the weight down. When it gave me the bill of materials: 15 rolls of filament, I was flabbergasted. This thing was going to weigh 9-10kg, cost over $150, and I had absolutely no idea if it was going to fit together and work at all. Stuff it, you only live once. I clicked order, and a few days later my massive box arrived, and I got to work.

![The giant nutcracker’s 3D-printed legs, with black boots, silver knees and white upper legs, standing beside a woven planter](images/ben-nutcracker-3d-print.jpg)

I have done some 3D modelling in the past; I’ve created 4-500 3D floorplans of houses, but I had no interest in learning what’s required to assemble a 1.6m nutcracker. I took a bet that Claude did, and I’ve trusted it with my wallet. We have some customers who are experimenting with AI to [build their own internal applications](https://peti.to/posts/build-vs-buy-bill-moved/). Some of them are coming to us to help put the rigour around them to [get them production ready](https://peti.to/posts/millions-a-month-to-save-600k/), but increasingly, I think some will not, and our business is going to change rapidly over the next 24 months.

What happens when someone decides to let AI rewrite their core business SaaS product internally in Python, and [no human knows how it works](https://peti.to/posts/would-it-count-as-legacy/), and something goes wrong? Who do you call when things stop working, and everything grinds to a halt? Of course you turn to the same AI that wrote it, but one thing I’ve seen again and again with AI is that it [will always add something when asked, and almost never remove anything](https://peti.to/posts/ai-never-tells-you-its-done/). This means more lines of Python, more bloat, more spaghetti that may not have a simple fix without a large rewrite. This means testing, delays, while panic sets in, deadlines keep passing, and customers keep screaming at you.

I’m making a $150 bet on my giant nutcracker; how big is your bet going to be?
